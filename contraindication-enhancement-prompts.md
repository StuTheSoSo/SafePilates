# Contraindication & Health Concern Enhancement Prompts

This document is a standalone guide for improving the SafePilates app with stronger contraindication, modification, and health-concern guidance.

---

## 1. Highlight visible contraindications on exercises
**Status:** ✅ Implemented
**Goal:** Make risk factors obvious before an instructor or user commits to a movement.

**Prompt:**
> For every exercise, add a clearly labeled contraindication block at the top of the detail page. Use phrasing such as "Not recommended for..." or "Use caution with..." and list the specific concerns in short, bullet-style text.

**Example:**
> Not recommended for clients with osteoporosis, pregnancy, or neck instability.

---

## 2. Add immediate modification guidance
**Status:** ✅ Implemented
**Goal:** Give practical, safe alternatives without forcing the instructor to search elsewhere.

**Prompt:**
> After the contraindication warning, add one or two quick modification cues. Use actionable language like "Modify by...", "Use support for...", or "Alternative: ...".

**Example:**
> Modify by reducing range of motion and keeping the rib cage supported.

---

## 3. Create condition-specific adaptation cues
**Status:** ✅ Implemented
**Goal:** Tie common health concerns directly to the exercise description.

**Prompt:**
> For each exercise, add specific adaptation notes for common conditions. Use statements such as "If the client has low back pain, keep the pelvis neutral" or "For shoulder instability, avoid overhead reach and keep the shoulders long." Keep each cue short and precise.

**Example:**
> If the client has knee pain, avoid deep flexion and perform the movement with a smaller range of motion.

---

## 4. Add a "What to avoid" section
**Status:** ✅ Implemented
**Goal:** Tell the user exactly what risky movement or alignment to avoid.

**Prompt:**
> Add a short "What to avoid" bullet or sentence under the exercise details. Use specific movement language such as "Avoid spinal compression", "Do not lock the knees", or "Avoid pressing the head into the mat." This should be distinct from the general description.

**Example:**
> Avoid arching the lower back or pressing the head into the mat.

---

## 5. Add "Alternative exercise" recommendations
**Status:** ✅ Implemented
**Goal:** Provide a safe substitute when the main exercise is contraindicated.

**Prompt:**
> For exercises that present meaningful risk, add an "Alternative" suggestion. The substitute should address the same objective with less risk. Keep it simple and instructor-friendly.

**Example:**
> Alternative: Bridge with support for clients who cannot tolerate full rolling motions.

---

## 6. Improve health-concern search and filtering
**Goal:** Enable users to find safe exercises by condition rather than by exercise name.

**Prompt:**
> Define a set of common concerns that can be searched or filtered, such as pregnancy, low back pain, osteoporosis, shoulder instability, and knee issues. For each concern, categorize exercises as "Safe", "Modify", or "Avoid." Use consistent terms so the search results are easy to interpret.

**Example:**
> Pregnancy: safe with pelvic support and no prone pressure. Avoid advanced supine abdominal crunches.

---

## 7. Add a "Risk summary" or quick guide page
**Goal:** Make it easy for an instructor to compare conditions and decide on modifications.

**Prompt:**
> Create a dedicated reference page or section listing common conditions with short summaries. Include "What to avoid", "Safe focuses", and "Key modifications". Use bullet points for fast reading.

**Example:**
> Low back pain: avoid excessive spinal flexion, maintain neutral pelvis, prioritize slow, controlled movement.

---

## 8. Use stronger safety-focused teaching cues
**Status:** ✅ Implemented
**Goal:** Encourage safer, more mindful practice through precise instruction.

**Prompt:**
> Add teaching cues that emphasize alignment and sensation, e.g. "Keep the neck long" or "Draw the navel toward the spine." Pair each with a safety reminder when appropriate: "If you feel sharp pain, stop immediately."

**Example:**
> Keep the shoulder blades wide and the ribs soft. Stop if there is any sharp pain in the neck.

---

## 9. Highlight concern keywords in search results and details
**Status:** ✅ Implemented
**Goal:** Boost scanability for at-risk language like contraindications and modifications.

**Prompt:**
> Highlight keywords such as "avoid", "modify", "alternative", "pregnancy", "osteoporosis", and "low back pain" in search results and exercise detail pages. Use a visual style that stands out without overwhelming the layout.

---

## 10. Add instructor-focused notes for special populations
**Status:** ✅ Implemented
**Goal:** Support Pilates teachers with an immediate clinical context.

**Prompt:**
> Add a short instructor note for each exercise when special populations are involved. Use language like "Use with caution for..." or "Best for clients who...". This should help instructors make quick decisions.

**Example:**
> Use with caution for clients who have poor scapular stability.
