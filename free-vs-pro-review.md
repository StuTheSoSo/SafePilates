Loss aversion (strongest trigger for professionals)

Results page, locked condition: instead of "Upgrade to Pro", show the exact count — "Your client has 7 specific exercise modifications. You're missing all of them." Specificity converts far better than abstraction.
When a user taps a locked condition chip on the home screen, the current paywall is generic. Replace with: "Clients with fibromyalgia walk into studios every week. See exactly what to avoid."
Completion / partial progress

Settings page: show "41 of 88 conditions unlocked" with a progress bar. Partial completion creates a persistent itch to complete. This is the same mechanic that makes Duolingo streaks work.
Identity framing ("I am a professional")

Onboarding or first launch: ask "Are you a Pilates instructor or student?". If instructor, show professional copy everywhere. This small self-identification makes professional pricing feel personally justified.
The pro gate on the Clients page already says "Pro feature" — change the framing to "How professional instructors manage clients" not "This requires Pro."
Risk / liability framing

Upgrade page: "One injury from a client whose condition you weren't briefed on costs more than a decade of Pro." This reframes $49/year as insurance, not a subscription. Professionals respond strongly to liability framing.
Peer norming

Upgrade page: "The safety reference trusted by mat and apparatus Pilates instructors." Even without real numbers, belonging to a professional peer group is a strong motivator.
Micro-commitment (foot-in-the-door)

After a free results check: surface a soft "Save this to a client profile?" CTA. The user says yes → hits the Pro gate → but they've already committed to the action. Commitment at the point of intent converts better than commitment at the point of pricing.
Anchoring

Already planned: "Less than one cancelled session". Reinforce it visually — show the math: "$49/year = $0.95/week". Trivializing the cost with a per-week figure is a classic anchor.
Default bias

On the upgrade page, the annual plan should be visually pre-selected (highlighted, larger), not equal weight to monthly. The current layout shows both equally — most users will take the default.
